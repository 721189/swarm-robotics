\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Autonomous drone swarms promise transformative capabilities in search and rescue, environmental monitoring, infrastructure inspection, and defense~\cite{chung2018survey,bravo2023swarm}.
In all of these missions, a central computational problem recurs: \emph{which robot should do which task?}
Centralized assignment is brittle---it introduces a single point of failure, a communication bottleneck at the leader, and a scalability ceiling that grows with fleet size~\cite{gerkey2004formal,korsah2015comprehensive}.
A standard decentralized answer is the \textbf{Consensus-Based Bundle Algorithm} (CBBA) of Choi, Brunet, and How~\cite{choi2009}, a consensus auction that provably converges to a conflict-free, 50\%-optimal assignment for scoring functions satisfying the Diminishing Marginal Gains (DMG) property, under the paper's stated communication assumptions.
CBBA and its descendants are widely adopted across multi-robot research prototypes~\cite{nestinger2018cbbaSurvey,verma2021cbbaApplications}.

\subsection{Motivation: The Communication Gap}
CBBA's guarantees, however, rest on an implicit physical assumption: that agents exchange their bid ledgers \emph{perfectly, instantaneously, and simultaneously}.
No real swarm satisfies this assumption.
A fleet of $N$ drones sharing one half-duplex radio channel cannot transmit concurrently; uncoordinated transmissions collide; packets are dropped by fading and interference; and information about a teammate's bid arrives only after a nonzero, topology-dependent delay~\cite{tardioli2019grounding,mott2020communication}.
In contested environments---the very settings where swarms are most valuable---adversarial jamming and range limits widen this gap further.

Closing it naively is dangerous.
Serializing CBBA's message exchanges onto a shared channel introduces \emph{information staleness}: each agent acts on beliefs about teammates that are outdated by up to one full communication frame, and stale beliefs interact destructively with CBBA's conflict-resolution logic.
Worse, when practitioners add the standard engineering safeguard of \emph{belief expiry}---discarding telemetry older than a validity horizon $T_{\mathrm{val}}$, so that lost or departed agents are forgotten~\cite{mott2020communication}---we find that staleness can become fatal:
if a full TDMA cycle $N\tau$ exceeds $T_{\mathrm{val}}$, every agent's belief about every teammate expires before it can be refreshed; cascading allocation erasures then race against re-learning, and consensus becomes \emph{structurally unreachable}.
To our knowledge, this failure mode---a \emph{synchronization--staleness phase transition} governed by the ratio $N\tau / T_{\mathrm{val}}$---has not been identified in the CBBA literature, despite belief-expiry heuristics being common practice in deployed systems.
\subsection{Our Approach}
We study a communication-constrained, bundle-size-one CBBA variant,
which we refer to as \textbf{TDMA-CBBA}, in which:
(i) agents transmit exclusively within assigned Time Division Multiple Access slots of duration $\tau$, eliminating intra-frame collisions by construction;
(ii) transmissions are subject to independent stochastic packet loss on both transmit and receive legs;
(iii) each agent maintains a time-stamped ledger of peers' bids and allocations, filtered through a belief-validity horizon $T_{\mathrm{val}}$.
Rather than treating the radio as an afterthought, we place the interplay between scheduling delay ($N\tau$) and belief staleness ($T_{\mathrm{val}}$) at the center of both the analysis and the experimental design.

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Convergence theory under scheduled, lossy communication (Thm.~\ref{thm:noiseless}).}
    We prove that TDMA-CBBA converges to a conflict-free assignment within
    $T_{TDMA} \leq T_{ideal} + N\tau K$
    for $K$ auction rounds, provided one full TDMA frame fits within the belief-validity horizon, $N\tau \leq T_{\mathrm{val}}$ (Thm.~\ref{thm:noiseless}(i)).
    To our knowledge this is the first convergence result for CBBA under slotted medium access with explicit belief expiry.
    \item \textbf{Single-task consensus and a conditional optimality statement (Prop.~\ref{prop:optimality}).}
    Under bounded communication delay with monotonically merged ledgers, we prove convergence to a conflict-free assignment and state precisely under what conditions the resulting utility can be compared against the optimal fixed-score assignment; no unconditional $50\%$ guarantee is claimed for the implemented single-task variant.
    \item \textbf{A synchronization--staleness phase transition (Sec.~\ref{sec:theory-noiseless}(ii), Thm.~\ref{thm:noiseless}).}
    We prove that when $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}}$, belief expiry renders global consensus unreachable, and we validate this impossibility result empirically: trials beyond the transition exhibit consensus rates collapsing toward zero while sub-transition trials converge reliably.
    This yields an actionable design rule---scale the validity horizon with the TDMA frame.
    \item \textbf{An open-source benchmark with rigorous statistics.}
    We release a fully deterministic headless benchmark (324 configurations, 15 independent seeds each; swarm sizes 10--50, packet loss up to 30\%, three slot durations, four baseline radio models), formal metric definitions, censoring-aware survival analysis, factorial regression, and publication-quality figures.
    All code, raw data, and analysis pipelines are public.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/721189/swarm-robotics}}
\end{enumerate}
