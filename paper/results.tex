\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

We report results from the released corpus of $1{,}620$ TDMA-CBBA trials (Sec.~\ref{sec:test-matrix}).
All figures are generated directly from the raw CSV by the open-source analysis pipeline; no manual curation is involved.

The aggregate PDR of $0.937$ matches the analytic link-success expectation under the two-stage loss model of Sec.~\ref{sec:method-loss} averaged over the loss sweep, providing an end-to-end sanity check that the radio simulator behaves as designed.

\subsection{Convergence Behavior}
Fig.~\ref{fig:mtc_vs_swarm} shows MTC against swarm size.
Consensus time grows approximately linearly with $n$ at low loss, consistent with the scheduling-delay term $N\tau K$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:convergence}: each additional drone both lengthens the frame and adds one ledger to synchronize.
At larger swarms and higher loss rates the curve separates into a converging branch and a saturated branch pinned at the 50\,s horizon---the two regimes predicted by Theorems~\ref{thm:convergence} and \ref{thm:impossibility}, respectively.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{mtc_vs_swarm_size.png}
\caption{Mean time to consensus vs.\ swarm size (error bars: std.\ across trials). Beyond the staleness threshold $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}} = 2$\,s of Thm.~\ref{thm:impossibility}, MTC saturates at the mission horizon, marking the synchronization--staleness phase transition.}
\label{fig:mtc_vs_swarm}
\end{figure}

The convergence CDF (Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence_curve}) makes this bimodality explicit: the empirical distribution exhibits a steep rise at short times (sub-threshold trials) followed by a long flat plateau (censored trials above threshold), rather than the unimodal tail a purely stochastic slowdown would produce.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering

\caption{Empirical CDF of time to consensus. The steep initial rise corresponds to sub-threshold configurations; the plateau is the population of censored trials predicted by Thm.~\ref{thm:impossibility}.}
\label{fig:convergence_curve}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Packet Delivery Ratio}
Fig.~\ref{fig:pdr_vs_pl} shows PDR degrading gracefully and near-linearly with nominal loss.
PDR remains above $93\%$ at 15\% transmit-leg loss and approximately $85\%$ at 30\% loss, matching the closed-form link success probability $(1 - p_{\mathrm{tx}})(1 - p_{\mathrm{rx}})$ to within sampling error---evidence that the slotted discipline itself introduces no additional delivery penalty.

\subsection{Factor Correlations}
Pearson correlations between experimental factors and outcomes (Table~\ref{tab:correlation}) quantify the coupling structure:
packet loss dominates PDR ($r = -0.993$, essentially deterministic given the closed-form channel model);
swarm size drives consensus time ($r = 0.723$), as predicted by the linear term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:convergence-bound};
and swarm size correlates moderately with CE ($r = 0.424$), reflecting that larger teams cover more objectives before horizon effects intervene.

Boxplots show MTC strongly right-skewed (censored mass at 50\,s), CE concentrated near its ceiling with a lower tail produced by high-loss trials, and SCI tightly distributed around $\approx 0.48$, indicating stable flocking geometry once assignments exist.

\subsection{Summary}
Three findings emerge.
(1)~Below the staleness threshold, TDMA-CBBA converges reliably, with cost scaling linearly in swarm size and slot duration exactly as Theorem~\ref{thm:convergence} predicts.
(2)~Above the threshold, consensus fails \emph{structurally} rather than statistically---validating Theorem~\ref{thm:impossibility} and motivating horizon scaling as a design rule.
(3)~Per-link delivery follows the analytic channel model independently of swarm logic, confirming clean separation between the communication layer and the allocation layer.
