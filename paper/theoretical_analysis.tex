\section{Theoretical Analysis}\label{sec:theory}

This section develops the paper's formal guarantees under a communication model that is honest about stochastic loss.
Section~\ref{sec:theory-age} defines the \emph{belief-age process}---the exact quantity whose interplay with scheduling produces both the guarantees and the failure mode.
Sections~\ref{sec:theory-noiseless}--\ref{sec:theory-stoch} then state what does and does not survive: a deterministic convergence guarantee in the noiseless regime, a probabilistic guarantee under stochastic loss, and a structural impossibility beyond the staleness threshold.
Section~\ref{sec:theory-worst} treats optimality under explicitly frozen scores, and Section~\ref{sec:theory-complexity} analyzes complexity.

Throughout, $q = (1-p_{\mathrm{tx}})(1-p_{\mathrm{rx}})$ denotes the per-frame end-to-end delivery success probability on a link, and all probabilistic statements are with respect to the independent loss process of Sec.~\ref{sec:comm-model}.

\subsection{The Belief-Age Process}\label{sec:theory-age}

The central object is not the delay of a single packet but the \emph{age} of each agent's belief about each peer.

\begin{definition}[Inter-success gap]\label{def:gap}
Fix a directed link $(j \to i)$. Agent $d_j$ transmits once per TDMA frame (period $N\tau$); each attempt survives the sender leg with probability $u = 1 - p_{\mathrm{tx}}$ and is decoded by $d_i$ with probability $r = 1 - p_{\mathrm{rx}}$, independently across frames and links, giving per-frame success $q = ur$.
The \emph{inter-success gap} $G_{ij}$ is the elapsed time between consecutive packets from $d_j$ received by $d_i$: it takes values $G_{ij} = k\,N\tau$ ($k \geq 1$) with
\begin{equation}
    \Pr[G_{ij} = k N\tau] = (1-q)^{\,k-1}\, q,
    \label{eq:gap-geom}
\end{equation}
and expected value $\mathbb{E}[G_{ij}] = N\tau / q$.
\end{definition}

A belief about $d_j$ held by $d_i$ expires exactly when the current gap exceeds the validity horizon: $\mathbb{1}[G_{ij} > T_{\mathrm{val}}]$.
The geometry of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap-geom} is captured by a single integer.

\begin{definition}[Loss-immunity budget]\label{def:immunity}
The \emph{loss-immunity budget} of the configuration $(N, \tau, T_{\mathrm{val}})$ is
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda \;=\; \left\lceil \frac{T_{\mathrm{val}}}{N\tau} \right\rceil - 1 \;\geq\; 0,
    \label{eq:immunity}
\end{equation}
the maximum number of consecutively lost frames a link can absorb while the belief remains valid up to the moment of refresh.
Equivalently, expiry occurs on a link iff its gap spans $\Lambda + 2$ or more frame periods.
\end{definition}

Two per-link quantities follow immediately from Definitions~\ref{def:gap}--\ref{def:immunity}:

\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{\pi_{\mathrm{exp}} = (1-q)^{\Lambda+1}}_{\text{expiry prob.\ per refresh opportunity}}
    \qquad
    \underbrace{\frac{N\tau}{q} \leq T_{\mathrm{val}}}_{\text{mean-gap (freshness) condition}},
    \label{eq:pi-exp}
\end{equation}
where the second is equivalent to $q \geq N\tau / T_{\mathrm{val}}$.
The budget $\Lambda$ cleanly unifies the regimes discussed in this paper:
$\Lambda = 0$ (i.e., $N\tau \leq T_{\mathrm{val}} < 2N\tau$) means a single lost frame already triggers expiry;
larger $\Lambda$ buys robustness linearly in $T_{\mathrm{val}}$;
and the D-TDMA extension of Sec.~\ref{sec:dtdma}, by stretching granted slots to $2\tau$, at most doubles the effective frame period and therefore \emph{halves} the immunity available near threshold.

This decomposition also separates the two loss mechanisms functionally:
$p_{\mathrm{tx}}$ enters only through the update rate $u = 1-p_{\mathrm{tx}}$ (it suppresses whole transmissions, inflating gaps toward the mean condition), whereas $p_{\mathrm{rx}}$ acts as pure per-link erasure inside $q$.
Empirically this split is exact in our simulator (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}): measured per-link PDR equals $r = 1 - p_{\mathrm{rx}}$ conditioned on transmission, and measured successful-update frequency equals $u$.

\begin{lemma}[Monotone ledger merging]\label{lem:monotone}
For every objective $o_j$ and agent $i$, the stored bid $\mathcal{B}_i^{(t)}(o_j)$ is non-decreasing in $t$ except when reset by ghost pruning, and merging never decreases either party's bid.
Moreover $\mathcal{W}_i(o_j)$ always names the agent holding the bid $\mathcal{B}_i(o_j)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both the reception merge (adopt only strictly greater bids) and the self-bid update (claim only when $c_{ij} > \mathcal{B}_i(o_j)$) write values strictly greater than the stored value; winner entries are copied together with bids from the same packet, so bid--winner pairs remain consistent.
Ghost pruning resets a bid to $0$ only when its believed winner has left the perceived swarm---which under expiry-free operation cannot be caused by scheduling alone, only by genuine attrition.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Guarantee I: Noiseless Regime (Deterministic)}\label{sec:theory-noiseless}

We first state what holds exactly, without probabilistic qualification.

\begin{theorem}[Noiseless convergence]\label{thm:noiseless}
Assume DMG scores (A1), slotted access with $p_{\mathrm{tx}} = p_{\mathrm{rx}} = 0$ (hence $q = 1$, $G_{ij} = N\tau$ deterministically), and the freshness condition
\begin{equation}
    N\tau \;\leq\; T_{\mathrm{val}} .
    \label{eq:noiseless-cond}
\end{equation}
Then no belief ever expires, ghost pruning never fires from scheduling alone, and TDMA-CBBA reaches a conflict-free consensus within
\begin{equation}
    T_{TDMA} \;\leq\; T_{ideal} + N\tau K ,
    \label{eq:noiseless-bound}
\end{equation}
where $T_{ideal}$ and $K$ are canonical CBBA's convergence time and round count on the same instance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $q=1$ every gap equals exactly $N\tau$, so under \eqref{eq:noiseless-cond} beliefs are refreshed strictly before expiry; perceived swarms always contain all agents and pruning is inert.
Each logical CBBA round requires one full frame of serialization, stretching round time from $\Delta t$ to $N\tau$; monotone merging (Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}) ensures each delivered ledger makes irreversible progress toward the unique fixed point that ideal CBBA attains after $K$ rounds~\cite{choi2009}.
Summing yields \eqref{eq:noiseless-bound}; conflict-freeness follows from bid--winner consistency.
\end{proof}

This is the precise, deterministic content of Contribution~1: it replaces the earlier claim of bounded delay under stochastic loss---which cannot hold since $(1-q)^k > 0$ for every $k$---with a guarantee that is unconditional exactly when loss is absent.

\subsection{Guarantee II: Stochastic Regime (In Probability)}\label{sec:theory-stoch}

Under genuine loss ($q < 1$), gaps are unbounded random variables (Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap-geom}); deterministic bounds are impossible, and over an unbounded horizon even rare expiry events occur almost surely.
The correct statement is therefore \emph{episode-level, in probability}.

\begin{definition}[Consensus episode]\label{def:episode}
An episode is a contiguous interval of $F = \lceil K \rceil$ frames during which the swarm attempts consensus from an initial ledger state.
It is \emph{expiry-free} if no link experiences $G_{ij} > T_{\mathrm{val}}$ anywhere in the interval.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Probabilistic episode consensus]\label{thm:probabilistic}
Assume A1, slotted access, $0 < q < 1$, and $\Lambda \geq 0$ per Eq.~\eqref{eq:immunity}.
Then TDMA-CBBA reaches a conflict-free consensus within $T_{ideal} + N\tau K$ with probability at least
\begin{equation}
    \Pr[\text{consensus in episode}] \;\geq\; \bigl(1 - \pi_{\mathrm{exp}}\bigr)^{\,n(n-1)\,F},
    \qquad \pi_{\mathrm{exp}} = (1-q)^{\Lambda+1},
    \label{eq:prob-bound}
\end{equation}
where $n = |\mathcal{D}|$ and $F$ is the episode length in frames (Def.~\ref{def:episode}).
The bound is tight in the sense that a single expiry event on any link invalidates the precondition of Theorem~\ref{thm:noiseless} for that episode.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the episode being expiry-free.
There are $n(n{-}1)$ directed links, each observing at most $F$ refresh opportunities subject to independent loss; by Eq.~\eqref{eq:pi-exp} a given link stays expiry-free across the episode with probability $(1-\pi_{\mathrm{exp}})^{F}$, and links are independent, giving the exponent.
Conditional on expiry-freedom every belief is refreshed before purging exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:noiseless}, so the same monotone-progress argument delivers consensus within the same time bound.
Tightness follows from Sec.~\ref{sec:method-auction}(a): one purged belief removes its peer from the perceived swarm and activates ghost pruning for the objectives it held.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:prob-bound} is the probabilistic reliability boundary promised in the introduction: failure probability scales exponentially better in the immunity budget $\Lambda$ (i.e., in $T_{\mathrm{val}}/N\tau$), and exponentially worse in loss $(1-q)$, network size $n$, and episode length $F$.
Because $\Lambda+1$ is an integer step function of $T_{\mathrm{val}}/N\tau$, crossing the threshold jumps $\pi_{\mathrm{exp}}$ from near zero to order unity---explaining why experiments exhibit a sharp empirical transition rather than soft degradation.

\begin{remark}[Permanent consistency under stochastic loss]\label{rem:permanent}
Definition~\ref{def:eventual} demands agreement for all $t \geq T_{\mathrm{cons}}$.
Under $q<1$ with independent losses this cannot be guaranteed over an infinite horizon: arbitrarily long failure streaks occur almost surely, eventually triggering expiry and re-entering the dynamics.
All stochastic guarantees here are therefore episode-granularity (Def.~\ref{def:episode}); permanent eventual consistency holds only in the noiseless regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:noiseless}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Structural Impossibility Beyond the Threshold}\label{sec:phase-transition}

\begin{theorem}[Impossibility for zero-immunity configurations]\label{thm:impossibility}
If $p_{\mathrm{tx}} = p_{\mathrm{rx}} = 0$ and $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}}$ (i.e., $\Lambda < 0$), then for every agent $i$ and peer $j \neq i$ the belief about $j$ expires before $j$'s next transmission.
Consequently each perceived swarm collapses to $\{i\}$ between receptions, ghost pruning erases all foreign winner entries every auction round, and no allocation survives a full frame: consensus (Def.~\ref{def:eventual}) is unreachable for any $N \geq 2$, deterministically, regardless of runtime.
For $q < 1$ with $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}}$ the collapse occurs with probability tending to one within $O(1)$ frames.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $q = 1$, consecutive transmissions of $d_j$ are spaced exactly $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}}$; just before each transmission, every earlier packet from $d_j$ has aged past $T_{\mathrm{val}}$ and is purged by (C3).
Hence $\mathcal{P}_i$ excludes $j$ whenever $d_i$ auctions, and rule (a) of Sec.~\ref{sec:method-auction} resets all winner entries belonging to $j$; symmetrically $j$ erases $i$'s wins.
Allocations created in one frame are destroyed in the next, cycling forever.
For $q < 1$: a gap $\leq T_{\mathrm{val}} < N\tau$ would require receiving two packets from $j$ within a single inter-transmission spacing---impossible under one-transmission-per-frame scheduling---so perceived swarms collapse identically, now with probability one over any window longer than a few frames.
\end{proof}

Together, Theorems~\ref{thm:noiseless}--\ref{thm:impossibility} locate the operating envelope:
the ratio $T_{\mathrm{val}} / N\tau$ determines whether the swarm sits in the deterministic regime ($q{=}1$, $\Lambda \geq 0$), the probabilistic regime ($q{<}1$, $\Lambda \geq 0$, success w.p.\ per Eq.~\eqref{eq:prob-bound}), or the collapsed regime ($\Lambda < 0$).
Section~\ref{sec:results} validates this three-regime picture with a sweep centered on $\Lambda = 0$.

\subsection{Optimality Under Frozen Scores}\label{sec:theory-worst}

A remaining question is whether stale bids degrade solution quality below CBBA's 50\% bound.
Because agents move while auctions run, scores are time-varying, and we are careful about what can be claimed.

\begin{proposition}[Optimality for frozen score profiles]\label{prop:optimality}
Fix an auction episode and let $c^{\circ}_{ij}$ denote the score profile at episode start, held fixed (frozen) for the duration of the episode; let $\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\circ}$ be the optimal conflict-free utility under $c^{\circ}$ and $\mathcal{U}$ the realized utility at TDMA-CBBA termination within that episode, measured under the same frozen profile.
If the episode is expiry-free (Def.~\ref{def:episode}) and scores satisfy DMG, then
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{U} \geq 0.5\,\mathcal{U}^{\ast}_{\circ}.
    \label{eq:optimality-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on expiry-freedom, Theorem~\ref{thm:noiseless} applies verbatim to the frozen-score instance: TDMA serializes rounds but preserves CBBA's update semantics over the realized bid sequence.
Canonical CBBA attains at least half of optimal on any DMG instance regardless of bid arrival order~\cite{choi2009}; the terminal ledger state here is exactly such a fixed point, giving the bound.
\end{proof}

Two caveats delimit this result honestly.
First, it is \emph{conditional}: Theorem~\ref{thm:probabilistic} bounds the probability of its precondition.
Second, the \emph{tracking gap} between frozen and live scores grows with motion during the episode: since bids change by at most $v_{\max}\,\Delta_{\mathrm{age}}$ under age bound $\Delta_{\mathrm{age}}$ (here $\leq 2N\tau$ when $\Lambda \geq 0$), realized utility satisfies
$|\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{live}} - \mathcal{U}| \leq n\, v_{\max}\, 2N\tau$,
so frozen-score optimality transfers to live utility up to this additive drift term.
Empirical measurement of realized optimality against a Hungarian oracle~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} remains open (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion}); we therefore do not claim Eq.~\eqref{eq:optimality-bound}-style guarantees as an empirically validated headline.
