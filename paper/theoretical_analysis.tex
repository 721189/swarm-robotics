\section{Theoretical Analysis}\label{sec:theory}

This section develops the paper's formal guarantees under a communication model that is honest about stochastic loss.
Section~\ref{sec:theory-age} defines the \emph{belief-age process}---the exact quantity whose interplay with scheduling produces both the guarantees and the failure mode.
Sections~\ref{sec:theory-noiseless}--\ref{sec:theory-stoch} then state what does and does not survive: consensus definitions made precise, a deterministic guarantee in the noiseless regime, and an exact stochastic reliability bound that invokes no independence assumptions between overlapping loss windows.
Section~\ref{sec:theory-complexity} analyzes complexity.

Throughout, $q = (1-p_{\mathrm{tx}})(1-p_{\mathrm{rx}})$ denotes the per-frame end-to-end delivery success probability on a link, and all probabilistic statements are with respect to the independent loss process of Sec.~\ref{sec:comm-model}.

\subsection{The Belief-Age Process}\label{sec:theory-age}

The central object is not the delay of a single packet but the \emph{age} of each agent's belief about each peer.

\begin{definition}[Inter-success gap]\label{def:gap}
Fix a directed link $(j \to i)$. Agent $d_j$ transmits once per TDMA frame (period $N\tau$); each attempt survives the sender leg with probability $u = 1 - p_{\mathrm{tx}}$ and is decoded by $d_i$ with probability $r = 1 - p_{\mathrm{rx}}$, independently across frames and links, giving per-frame success $q = ur$.
The \emph{inter-success gap} $G_{ij}$ is the elapsed time between consecutive packets from $d_j$ received by $d_i$: it takes values $G_{ij} = k\,N\tau$ ($k \geq 1$) with
\begin{equation}
    \Pr[G_{ij} = k N\tau] = (1-q)^{\,k-1}\, q,
    \label{eq:gap-geom}
\end{equation}
and expected value $\mathbb{E}[G_{ij}] = N\tau / q$.
\end{definition}

A belief about $d_j$ held by $d_i$ expires exactly when the current gap exceeds the validity horizon: $\mathbb{1}[G_{ij} > T_{\mathrm{val}}]$.
The geometry of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap-geom} is captured by a single integer.

\begin{definition}[Loss-immunity budget]\label{def:immunity}
The \emph{loss-immunity budget} of the configuration $(N,\,\tau,\,T_{\mathrm{val}})$ is
\[
\Lambda =
\left\lfloor \frac{T_{\mathrm{val}}}{N\tau}\right\rfloor-1.
\]
Configurations with $\Lambda\geq0$ admit at least one valid inter-transmission interval, whereas $\Lambda=-1$ corresponds to
\[
N\tau>T_{\mathrm{val}},
\]
for which even a successful packet cannot remain valid until the next scheduled transmission opportunity from the same peer. Equivalently, $\Lambda$ is the maximum number of consecutive frame losses a link can absorb while every belief remains valid until refreshed.
\end{definition}

Two per-link quantities follow immediately from Definitions~\ref{def:gap}--\ref{def:immunity}:

\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{\pi_{\mathrm{exp}} = (1-q)^{\Lambda+1}}_{\text{expiry prob.\ per refresh opportunity}}
    \qquad
    \underbrace{\frac{N\tau}{q} \leq T_{\mathrm{val}}}_{\text{mean-gap (freshness) condition}},
    \label{eq:pi-exp}
\end{equation}
where the second is equivalent to $q \geq N\tau / T_{\mathrm{val}}$.
The D-TDMA extension of Sec.~\ref{sec:dtdma}, by stretching granted slots to $2\tau$, at most doubles the effective frame period and therefore \emph{halves} the immunity available near threshold.

This decomposition separates the two loss mechanisms functionally:
$p_{\mathrm{tx}}$ enters only through the update rate $u = 1-p_{\mathrm{tx}}$ (it slows refresh),
while $p_{\mathrm{rx}}$ enters only through $r = 1-p_{\mathrm{rx}}$ (it corrupts otherwise timely refreshes).

\subsection{Consensus Definitions and the Noiseless Regime}\label{sec:theory-noiseless}

A recurring source of confusion in the empirical literature is conflating two distinct events; we define both precisely and never mix them.

\begin{definition}[First-passage agreement]\label{def:firstpassage}
$T_{\mathrm{fp}} = \inf\{ t : \mathcal{L}_i(t) = \mathcal{L}_j(t) \;\forall i,j \}$ is the first time all live ledgers coincide.
It is a snapshot property: it says nothing about any later instant.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Verified consensus]\label{def:verified}
The system achieves \emph{verified consensus} if agreement holds continuously over an interval of length $T_{\mathrm{val}}$: there exists $t_0$ such that $\mathcal{L}_i(t) = \mathcal{L}_j(t)\;\forall i,j$ for all $t \in [t_0,\, t_0 + T_{\mathrm{val}}]$.
This is the operational guarantee a deployed swarm needs---assignments derived from ledgers that stay agreed for one validity horizon cannot silently diverge mid-execution.
An \emph{expiry-free episode} is one in which no belief expires before first-passage agreement.
\end{definition}

Our benchmark records $T_{\mathrm{fp}}$, a binary indicator of verified consensus (agreement persisting for $T_{\mathrm{val}}/\Delta t$ frames after first passage), and the count of \emph{regressions}: post-passage disagreement events caused by belief expiry.
The distinction is decisive for interpreting Sec.~\ref{sec:results}: a swarm may agree momentarily ``in passing'' while being structurally incapable of \emph{holding} agreement.

\begin{lemma}[Monotone ledger merging]\label{lem:monotone}
The gossip update rule of Algorithm~\ref{alg:tdmacbba} is monotone in the following sense. For each objective $o_j$, the bid entry stored by agent $d_i$ is nondecreasing over time and equals the maximum of all incoming bids for $o_j$ that $d_i$ has decoded, provided no stored entry is pruned by belief expiry:
\begin{equation}
    b^{(i)}_j(t{+}) \;=\; \max\Bigl\{ b^{(i)}_j(t),\; \max_{k:\,\mathrm{msg}(k,j)\in \mathcal{M}_i(t)} b_{kj}\Bigr\},
    \label{eq:monotone-merge}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{M}_i(t)$ is $d_i$'s mailbox of non-expired messages. Consequently, whenever no expiry fires, every ledger entry is a running maximum of the delivered message stream, and entry values never decrease.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The receive handler adopts a sender's bid if and only if it is strictly larger than the local entry, and otherwise leaves the local entry unchanged (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-belief}); winner indices travel with the adopted bid, so the stored pair $(\mathrm{winner}, \mathrm{bid})$ changes only out of a strictly smaller stored bid. Because scores are constant throughout the episode (the frozen-score setting of Prop.~\ref{prop:optimality}), a larger received value can be overwritten only upward, never downward, whenever no pruning event removes it. Repeated application over the sequence of decoded messages yields exactly the running maximum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:monotone-merge}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Noiseless scheduling regimes]\label{thm:noiseless}
Suppose $p_{\mathrm{tx}} = p_{\mathrm{rx}} = 0$ (lossless TDMA), so every belief refreshes exactly once per frame period $N\tau$, and let scores be frozen for the episode (the frozen-score setting of Prop.~\ref{prop:optimality}). Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] \textbf{(Feasible regime, $\Lambda \geq 0$.)}
    If $N\tau \leq T_{\mathrm{val}}$, TDMA's round-robin gossip delivers, per round, exactly ideal CBBA's message set up to intra-round ordering; ledgers are monotone because pruning never fires (no packet age exceeds $N\tau \leq T_{\mathrm{val}}$).
    First-passage agreement therefore occurs within
    \begin{equation}
        T_{\mathrm{fp}} \;\leq\; T_{\mathrm{ideal}} + K \cdot N\tau,
        \label{eq:ttdma-bound}
    \end{equation}
    where $K$ is the number of gossip rounds ideal CBBA requires, and the agreement persists: verified consensus holds with probability one.
    \item[(ii)] \textbf{(Collapsed regime, $\Lambda = -1$.)}
    If $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}}$, every refresh arrives already expired: at decode time the sender entry is older than $T_{\mathrm{val}}$, so ghost pruning removes its wins before they influence the ledger. First-passage agreement may still occur transiently---and empirically does (Sec.~\ref{sec:results})---but it cannot persist: between two consecutive refreshes of any peer, that peer's entries expire and are pruned, forcing disagreement within every frame period. Verified consensus therefore has probability zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Under lossless TDMA each decoded packet has age at most $N\tau \leq T_{\mathrm{val}}$, so pruning never fires; each agent's ledger then evolves by the monotone merge of Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} under a fixed serialization of exactly the all-to-all message pattern per round. Convergence follows from the standard monotone-auction argument~\cite{choi2009}; each serialized round can lag the ideal round by at most one frame period $N\tau$, giving Eq.~\eqref{eq:ttdma-bound}, and with no expiry the terminal agreement never breaks, establishing verified consensus.
(ii) Consider any pair $(i,j)$ and two consecutive receptions by $i$ of packets from $j$, separated by exactly $N\tau > T_{\mathrm{val}}$. At every instant strictly between them, $i$'s belief about $j$ exceeds age $T_{\mathrm{val}}$, so ghost pruning removes $j$'s winning entries from $i$'s ledger. For global agreement to persist across this interval, $j$'s entries would have to be simultaneously present in $i$'s ledger---a contradiction unless they are absent everywhere, which conflicts with agreement requiring at least one real winner whenever $m \geq 1$. Hence agreement breaks within every frame period, and no interval of length $T_{\mathrm{val}}$ sustains it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Structural Impossibility Beyond the Threshold}\label{sec:phase-transition}

\begin{theorem}[Structural loss of persistent global consensus]
\label{thm:impossibility}
Consider the communication model of Sec.~\ref{sec:comm-model}
with exactly one transmission opportunity per agent per TDMA frame,
and the belief-expiry and ghost-pruning rules of
Sec.~\ref{sec:method-auction}. If
\[
N\tau>T_{\mathrm{val}},
\]
then every received message from a peer $d_j$ necessarily expires
before the next scheduled transmission opportunity of $d_j$.

Consequently, under the paper's ghost-pruning rule, a peer cannot
remain continuously present in another agent's perceived swarm`.
Therefore the persistent global-consensus condition of
Def.~\ref{def:eventual} cannot be maintained for the full swarm
when $N\geq2$.

This structural result is independent of packet-loss probability:
packet loss can only increase belief age and cannot reduce the
minimum inter-transmission interval below $N\tau$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every peer transmits at most once per TDMA frame, so consecutive
transmission opportunities from $d_j$ are separated by exactly
$N\tau$. Since
\[
N\tau>T_{\mathrm{val}},
\]
a message received from $d_j$ becomes older than
$T_{\mathrm{val}}$ before the next possible transmission from
$d_j$.

By (C3),the message is therefore removed from the receiver's
perceived swarm before it can be refreshed. The ghost-pruning rule
then removes ledger entries whose believed winner is that peer.Hence a peer cannot remain persistently represented in every
receiver's ledger, contradicting the persistent equality required
by Def.~\ref{def:eventual}. Packet loss cannot improve this condition,
because a failed transmission only increases the inter-success gap.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stochastic Loss: Exact Reliability Guarantees}\label{sec:theory-stoch}

With stochastic loss, refreshes arrive with geometrically distributed gaps (Def.~\ref{def:gap}) and expiry becomes a random event. Two errors must be avoided: treating overlapping loss windows as independent (they share samples), and claiming deterministic delay bounds that stochastic failure runs violate with nonzero probability. We make neither: the single-link probability below is \emph{exact}, and the network-level statement uses only the union bound, hence remains valid under arbitrary correlation in the loss process.

\begin{lemma}[Exact single-link non-expiry probability]\label{lem:run}
Fix a link and an audit window of $F$ refresh opportunities (frames). Let $s = \Lambda + 2$ denote the gap length (in frame periods) that triggers expiry; if $\Lambda = -1$ set $s = 1$ (every missed refresh expires the belief). With per-frame success $q$, the probability that \emph{no} expiry-causing failure run occurs in the window is
\begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{clean}}(F, s, q) \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{s-1} f_F(k),
    \label{eq:run-recursion}
\end{equation}
where $f_0(0) = 1$, $f_0(k) = 0$ for $k > 0$, and
$f_{t+1}(0) = q \sum_{k=0}^{s-1} f_t(k)$,
$f_{t+1}(k{+}1) = (1-q)\, f_t(k)$ for $0 \leq k < s{-}1$.
This recursion counts failure runs exactly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$f_t(k)$ is the probability that after $t$ attempts no run of $s$ consecutive failures has occurred and the trailing failure streak has length exactly $k < s$. A success resets the streak to zero (weight $q$, summed over all safe states); a failure extends it by one (weight $1-q$); reaching streak length $s$ leaves the safe state space and corresponds precisely to an expiry. Summing the mass over safe terminal states yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:run-recursion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Expiry-free episode probability]\label{thm:probabilistic}
Assume the Diminishing Marginal Gains (DMG) scoring property, slotted access,$0<q<1$,and
\[
\Lambda =
\left\lfloor \frac{T_{\mathrm{val}}}{N\tau}\right\rfloor-1
\geq 0.
\]
Let the episode consist of $F$ TDMA frames. For any directed
peer-to-peer link, the probability that at least one belief-expiry
event occurs during the episode is at most
\[
\pi_{\mathrm{exp}} =
(1-q)^{\Lambda+1}.
\]
Under the independence assumptions of Sec.~\ref{sec:comm-model},
the probability that no directed link experiences expiry during the
episode is
\[
P_{\mathrm{no-expiry}}
=
\left(1-\pi_{\mathrm{exp}}\right)^{N(N-1)F}.
\]
Thus
\[
P_{\mathrm{no-expiry}}
\geq
\left[
1-(1-q)^{\Lambda+1}
\right]^{N(N-1)F}.
\]
This result is a reliability bound on the belief-management process;
it does not by itself imply that consensus is reached within
$T_{\mathrm{ideal}}+N\tau K$ under stochastic packet loss.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For one directed link, a belief expires only after at least
$\Lambda+1$ consecutive transmission opportunities fail to produce a
refresh before the validity horizon. Since each opportunity succeeds
with probability $q$, the probability of such a run is
$(1-q)^{\Lambda+1}$.

There are $N(N-1)$ directed links and at most $F$ refresh opportunities
per link during the episode. Applying the union bound gives the stated
episode-level reliability expression. The bound concerns expiry only;
additional packet-delivery events may still delay the logical CBBA
updates required for consensus.
\end{proof}

A belief-expiry event invalidates the no-expiry precondition used by
the deterministic analysis; whether consensus is nevertheless reached
after such an event is an implementation- and trajectory-dependent
question evaluated empirically.

A remaining question is what can be claimed about solution quality when bids arrive stale.

Because agents move while auctions run,scores are time-varying,and we are careful about what can be claimed.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional optimality statement]
\label{prop:optimality}
For a fixed-score episode,ifthe implemented bundle-size-one
consensus auction terminates at a conflict-free allocation satisfying
the corresponding greedy exchange condition,then its utility can be
compared against the optimal assignment for that fixed instance.No general $50\%$ guarantee is claimed here unless the complete
conditions of the corresponding CBBA approximation theorem are
verified for the implemented update rule.

\end{proposition}
